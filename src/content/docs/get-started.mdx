## Choose your version

Owload is the same encrypted storage in every version: your files are encrypted on your device before they leave it, and the code that does it is [public](https://github.com/owload). The versions differ in **where the data lives and who runs the server**.

  <StartCard title="Web version with your own S3" href="#web-version-with-your-own-s3" icon="lock">The same app, your files in your own bucket. Free.</StartCard>
  <StartCard title="Self-hosted" href="../self-hosting/" icon="link">Run the whole of Owload on your own server, from Docker images.</StartCard>

### Compare

| | **Web version** | **Web version + your own S3** | **Self-hosted** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | Free | Free | Free (you pay for your server) |
| Where your encrypted files are | Owload's storage | **Your S3 bucket** | **Your S3 bucket** |
| Storage | 3 GB | As much as your bucket holds | As much as your bucket holds |
| Who runs the application | Owload | Owload | **You** |
| What you set up | Nothing | A bucket and an access key | A server, a database, sign-in (Keycloak), HTTPS |
| Updates | Automatic | Automatic | You pull a new version |
| Where your drive list and access rules live | Owload's database | Owload's database | **Your** database |

<Aside type="note" title="What is the same everywhere">
Passwords and encryption keys never leave your device, and none of the servers (ours or yours) can read your files. Choosing a version changes who stores the encrypted data and who runs the service, not how it is protected.
</Aside>

### Which one should I take?

- **You want to try it or just use it:** the [web version](#web-version). It takes a minute.
- **You want more than 3 GB, or want the data in your own storage:** the [web version with your own S3](#web-version-with-your-own-s3). Owload keeps running the service; your encrypted files sit in your bucket.
- **You want your files to survive the loss of a provider:** add [your own S3](#web-version-with-your-own-s3) at more than one provider, and keep a drive in several storages at once. See [Reliability](../reliability/).
- **You want nothing of yours on our servers at all, or you need it inside your own network:** [self-host](../self-hosting/). It is more work: you run the server, the database and the sign-in service yourself.

## Web version

The web version needs nothing to install: open Owload in your browser, sign up, and create a drive. Owload runs the service and stores your encrypted files.

- **3 GB of storage, free.** It counts the drives you own that are stored on Owload's storage.
- **Encryption on your device.** Files, file names and folders are encrypted in your browser before they are uploaded.
- **Sharing:** invite people with roles, or publish a read-only link. See [Access and roles](../sharing/#access-and-roles) and [Public drives and links](../sharing/#public-drives-and-links).
- **Opening and editing in the browser:** pictures, iPhone (HEIC) photos and videos can be viewed, and text and spreadsheet files edited. Other files are stored and downloaded. A **Media** section gathers photos and videos.

To start, follow [Create your first drive](#create-your-first-drive). Need more than 3 GB? Use [your own S3](#web-version-with-your-own-s3); paid plans with more space on Owload's storage are coming soon.

## Web version with your own S3

This is the web version with one difference: a drive's data is kept in **an S3 bucket that you own** instead of on Owload's storage. It is free, there is no storage limit from Owload, and you pay your storage provider, nobody else.

S3 is the common standard for cloud storage. Many providers offer it, and it can run on your own server: see [What S3 is](../storage/#what-s3-is) and [Which storage you can use](../storage/#which-storage-you-can-use).

- Files are encrypted in your browser first. What reaches the bucket is unreadable data.
- Owload's server writes and reads it **in your bucket** with an access key you give it, which it keeps encrypted. Use a key made for this one bucket.
- You need an S3-compatible storage that is **reachable from the internet** (Owload's server connects to it, not your browser) and a private bucket used only for Owload.

## Create your first drive

This guide takes you from nothing to a drive with files in it, using the [web version](#web-version). Using your own S3 or your own server changes only the storage step: see [Choose your version](#choose-your-version).

<Figure name="new-drive-form" />

<Steps>

1. **Create an account**

   Choose **Start free** on the main page and sign up with your email (or with Google, where offered). The account only identifies you; it does not unlock your files. Each drive is unlocked by its own password.

2. **Create a drive**

   A drive is a place for files with its own password, storage and access rules. Choose **New drive**, then:

   - give it a **name** and, if you like, a **description**;
   - set a strong **password**, and save it in a password manager;
   - choose **who can open it**: only you (private) is a safe start. You can change it later, see [Drives and access](../sharing/#access-and-roles);
   - keep the default **storage** (Owload's, 3 GB free), or enter your own S3 under **Custom**, see [Storage and your own S3](../storage/#storage-and-your-own-s3).

3. **Add files**

   Drag files into the drive, or use **Upload file**. Each shows two steps, **Encrypt** (on your device) and **Upload**. Create folders from the menu of the drive's area, and move things around by dragging. See [Files and folders](../using-drives/#files-and-folders).

4. **Open them**

   Click a picture, a video, a text file or a spreadsheet to open it in Owload; other files you download. Photos and videos also appear together in the **Media** section. See [Opening, previewing and editing](../using-drives/#opening-previewing-and-editing).

5. **Share, if you want to**

   Invite people by email with a role, or make the drive public for anyone with a link. In both cases, **give the drive's password separately**. See [Invitations and requests](../sharing/#invitations-and-requests) and [Public drives and links](../sharing/#public-drives-and-links).

6. **Close it when you are done**

   **Close** the drive, or **Close all drives**, to remove its keys from this browser. The next time, the password is asked again.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Keep the password safe">
The password encrypts the drive on your device. Owload never receives it, so it **cannot be recovered**.
</Aside>
